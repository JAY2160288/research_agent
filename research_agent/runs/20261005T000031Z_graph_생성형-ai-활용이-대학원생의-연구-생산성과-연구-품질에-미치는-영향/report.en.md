# Research Brief: 생성형 AI 활용이 대학원생의 연구 생산성과 연구 품질에 미치는 영향

> RQ — How does the use of generative AI affect graduate students' research productivity and research quality?

## 요약

이 브리프는 388편의 후보 중 평가를 거친 85편(주로 자기보고 설문·인터뷰, 일부 리뷰와 모델 성능 평가)을 근거로 한다. 합의된 부분은 대학원생과 대학생이 연구·글쓰기에서 GenAI의 효율성과 글쓰기 지원 효과를 긍정적으로 인식하면서도 정확성, 편향, 프라이버시, 과의존에 대한 우려를 함께 표명한다는 점이다. 기관·출판사 지침은 분절적이며 대학원 연구가 아니라 수업이나 출판 중심이고, 학문적 진실성의 경계는 모호하다. 상충하는 지점은 GenAI가 연구 진행을 가속하고 업무량을 줄이는지, 그리고 ChatGPT의 이점이 도전과제보다 큰지인데, 모집단과 과제가 달라 직접적 모순이라기보다 조건부 차이일 가능성이 크고 두 번째 상충은 근거가 얇다. 조건부 발견으로는 박사과정 10명의 단일 글쓰기 과제에서 반복적이고 상호작용이 많은 인간-AI 협업이 더 나은 수행과 연결되었으나 일반화하기 어렵다. 핵심 Gap은 대학원생의 생산성과 연구 품질이 실제로 측정된 적이 없고 인식 수준에 머물러 있다는 점이다. 또한 AI 지원 대학원 연구의 품질을 측정할 검증된 방법과 대학원·학위논문 특화 정책 근거도 없어 품질 하위질문(rq2)과 측정 하위질문(rq6)은 사실상 답하지 못했다.

## 1. 주제 재정의

- RQ: How does the use of generative AI affect graduate students' research productivity and research quality?
- 독립변수: Use of generative AI (e.g., large language models, ChatGPT) in research activities
- 종속변수: Research productivity, Research quality
- 대상: Graduate students (master's and doctoral students)
- 핵심 개념: generative AI, graduate students, research productivity, research quality, academic writing, higher education, AI-assisted research

## 2. 조사 계획

전략: Search 2022–present, since ChatGPT's release in late 2022 drives nearly all relevant work, prioritizing empirical studies (surveys, quasi-experiments, RCTs, mixed methods, interviews) and systematic reviews on graduate or postgraduate populations. Exclude papers focused solely on undergraduates or K-12, purely technical LLM benchmarks without student participants, and opinion pieces lacking evidence, unless they inform policy. Expect rq1 and rq2 to be thin on causal evidence specific to graduate students, since many studies mix student levels or rely on self-reports, and rq6 on measurement to be the thinnest.

- **rq1** How does generative AI use affect graduate students' research productivity, such as writing speed, output volume, and time efficiency?

- **rq2** How does generative AI use affect the quality of graduate students' research outputs, including writing quality, rigor, originality, and accuracy?

- **rq3** For which research tasks and stages (literature review, data analysis, coding, writing, revision) do graduate students use generative AI, and how do usage patterns relate to outcomes?

- **rq4** What mechanisms and moderators (AI literacy, prompting skill, discipline, English proficiency, supervisor guidance, self-efficacy) explain variation in the effects of generative AI on graduate research?

- **rq5** What risks and unintended effects of generative AI use arise for graduate researchers, including over-reliance, skill erosion, academic integrity violations, and bias?

- **rq6** How are productivity and quality of AI-assisted graduate research measured, and what policies or institutional guidelines govern its use?

## 4. 종합

### 합의

- Postgraduate and university students broadly report positive attitudes toward GenAI for research and writing, and they perceive efficiency, scope and writing-support benefits. All of this evidence is self-reported, with no objective measures of speed or output. [2, 5, 17, 19, 32] (근거 5편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.6)

- Students, including postgraduates, consistently raise concerns about accuracy, bias, privacy, over-reliance and effects on personal development. Postgraduates show greater awareness of ChatGPT's limitations than undergraduates. [3, 5, 17, 21, 25, 46] (근거 6편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.2)

- Institutional and publisher guidance on GenAI is fragmented and mostly coursework- or publication-oriented rather than graduate-research-specific. Students ask for training and clearer policy. [5, 25, 57, 58, 60, 76] (근거 6편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.0)

- The boundary of academic integrity in AI-assisted writing is ambiguous. Students disapprove of direct content generation but are ambivalent about subtler uses, and breaches hinge on disclosure and are hard to detect. Personal ethical beliefs, not policy awareness, predict misconduct perceptions and behaviour in a US sample, with no graduate-level split. [33, 56, 59] (근거 3편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.3)

- Reflective training can shift doctoral students' views of GenAI. A micro-curriculum produced de-enchantment, better ethical understanding, developed prompting methods and greater awareness of text ownership. This rests on self-reported reflections at one institution. [2, 4] (근거 2편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.0)

### 상충

- **Whether GenAI accelerates research progress and relieves workload.** (A 2편 vs B 2편)
  - A: Postgraduate research students and surveyed students report accelerated progress and improved efficiency from GenAI. [2, 5]
  - B: UK academics' survey suggests digitalisation may not deliver relief from overwork, and higher workload and time pressure drive students to use ChatGPT more, which is context rather than evidence of time saved. [11, 44]
  - 원인 가설: The populations differ: side B concerns academics and general university students rather than graduate researchers. Both sides use self-reported measures, and side B's abstracts are truncated. Perceived task-level speed-ups may coexist with rising overall workload expectations, so the two sets of findings may not directly contradict each other.

- **Whether the benefits of ChatGPT for postgraduate academic work outweigh the challenges.** (A 1편 vs B 1편)
  - A: Experienced postgraduate research users report enhanced research quality, critical thinking, confidence and autonomy. [2]
  - B: Postgraduate English-major students judged the challenges of ChatGPT in academic writing to outweigh the benefits, with concern about over-reliance. [3]
  - 원인 가설: Side A sampled research students with at least 4 months of ChatGPT use in research, whereas side B sampled English-major writers at one Vietnamese institution. Side B also focused explicitly on disadvantages. Both rely on small self-reported samples, so differences in experience, task and framing could explain the divergence.

### 조건부

- Better writing task performance is linked to iterative, highly interactive human-AI collaboration, as opposed to less interactive patterns. This is the only graduate-level link between usage pattern and outcome, and it rests on 10 doctoral students and a single writing task and tool. [1] (근거 1편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.0)

- Indirect language-learning evidence is separate from graduate research quality. A non-generative tool (Grammarly) improved postgraduate EFL post-test writing, and ChatGPT-assisted learning supported knowledge acquisition and motivation among second-language students while raising over-reliance concerns. These are language learners, not graduate researchers, and the studies do not show improved research writing. [12, 21] (근거 2편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.0)

- Self-efficacy and practical application are associated with higher perceived AI output quality, while critical appraisal was associated with lower perceived quality and lower self-efficacy. This is cross-sectional self-report in general university students. Whether critical appraisal helps or hurts actual outcomes is unresolved, as no cited paper shows it is beneficial. AI self-efficacy also predicted perceived critical-thinking and creativity support in undergraduates. [8, 10] (근거 2편 · 신뢰도 평균 4.0)

- Guidance appears to shape how GenAI is used. Prompt training and sample prompts shifted programming students' interaction profiles, and supervision roles are reported to shift toward strategic guidance. These are not graduate-research outcomes and are largely perceptual. [2, 34] (근거 2편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.0)

### 커버리지 메모

Evidence is strongest for student perceptions, ethical concerns and the policy landscape (rq5, rq6 policy), but mostly from self-report surveys of mixed or undergraduate samples. rq3 is thin: the only graduate-level usage-pattern-to-outcome link is a 10-person doctoral study of one writing task. Literature review, coding and data analysis are covered only by non-graduate, model-capability or truncated-abstract papers, and the doctoral intention study reports no outcomes. rq1 rests on perceived speed-ups in two self-report studies, with workload counter-context from non-graduate populations and truncated abstracts. rq2 and rq4 have little measured evidence on graduate research quality or on moderators such as English proficiency, supervisor guidance and discipline. Measurement in rq6 is only indirectly informed (reviewer ratings, recall/precision/hallucination metrics on model outputs, and perception scales), and no paper describes how productivity or quality of AI-assisted graduate research is measured. Policy evidence comes from coursework or publisher documents rather than graduate-school or thesis policies. Missing are longitudinal or experimental graduate studies with usage logs, time-on-task and objective quality or accuracy outcomes.

## 5. Research Gap

### G1. Graduate-student productivity is only ever perceived, never measured.

[2, 5, 11, 44] (근거 4편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.2)

Postgraduates report faster progress, while workload and time-pressure studies of academics and general students point the other way, and none of these studies track time-on-task or output. Whether task-level speed-ups survive rising workload expectations is untested in graduate researchers.

- 제안 RQ: Does GenAI access change measured time-on-task, output volume and total weekly research hours among master's and doctoral students, compared with matched peers without structured access?
- 방법: Pre-registered randomized or stepped-wedge field trial over one semester with two arms (supported GenAI access vs. usual practice). Primary outcomes are logged time-on-task and milestone completion, plus a weekly workload diary.
- 데이터: About 120-200 thesis-stage students from several graduate schools, recruited through graduate offices, with consented tool usage logs and supervisor-verified milestones.

### G2. Graduate use of GenAI for literature search and screening, coding and data analysis has not been studied with graduate users.

[6, 9, 18, 22] (근거 4편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.5)

Evidence comes from model-capability tests or reviews of generated text, or from non-graduate and truncated sources, so graduate outcomes on these tasks are unknown.

- 제안 RQ: How do graduate students' accuracy, hallucination rate and time differ on literature screening, citation retrieval and data-analysis coding tasks with and without GenAI?
- 방법: Within-subject crossover experiment in which each student completes matched tasks with and without GenAI. Outcomes are recall/precision against an expert gold standard, fabricated-reference rate, error rate in code, and time to completion.
- 데이터: 60-100 master's/doctoral students in two or three disciplines, with expert-built gold-standard review and analysis tasks and screen/log recording.

### G3. The only graduate-level link between usage pattern and outcome is a 10-person doctoral study of one writing task and one tool.

[1, 32, 34] (근거 3편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.3)

The doctoral intention study reports no outcomes, and guidance effects are shown only in programming students. Whether interactive use predicts better work across tasks, tools and guidance conditions is unknown.

- 제안 RQ: Do interaction patterns with GenAI predict thesis-chapter quality among doctoral students, and does prompt training shift those patterns?
- 방법: Longitudinal observational study with sequence analysis of interaction logs, linked to blinded rater scores of chapters. Nested within it, randomize prompt training vs. none and compare pattern distributions and quality.
- 데이터: Interaction logs and drafts from 80-150 doctoral students at multiple institutions, with consent and blinded raters from their fields.

### G4. No paper reports a validated way to measure the quality of AI-assisted graduate research.

[6, 33, 57, 58] (근거 4편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.2)

Existing quality evidence is reviewer ratings of generated articles or self-reports, and the policy sources address coursework or publishers rather than theses. Policy awareness did not affect behaviour in the one test, so policy effects on graduate work are unknown.

- 제안 RQ: Can a rubric for AI-assisted thesis and manuscript quality (accuracy, originality, citation integrity, disclosure) reach acceptable inter-rater reliability, and does it differ between graduate programs with and without explicit AI policies?
- 방법: Develop the rubric with a Delphi panel, then double-rate thesis chapters and compute inter-rater reliability. Compare scores across programs with and without thesis-specific AI policies.
- 데이터: A sample of 100-200 chapters or manuscripts from graduate schools, plus the programs' AI policy documents and supervisor panels.

### G5. It is unresolved whether critical appraisal of AI output helps or hurts real outcomes.

[4, 8, 10] (근거 3편 · 신뢰도 평균 3.7)

Appraisal lowered perceived output quality and self-efficacy in general students, and reflective training produced de-enchantment in doctoral students, but neither study measured actual error detection or work quality.

- 제안 RQ: Does appraisal training change doctoral students' detection of AI errors, the accuracy of their final text and their self-efficacy?
- 방법: Two-arm randomized experiment (appraisal training vs. generic tool training) on a research-writing task with seeded AI errors. Outcomes are error-detection rate, blinded quality score and self-efficacy.
- 데이터: About 100 doctoral students recruited through graduate schools, with a task containing known seeded errors.

## 7. 한계와 신뢰도

- Only titles, abstracts and metadata were read (no full texts), and several abstracts were truncated, so effect sizes, sample details and methods could not be verified.

- Search was limited to OpenAlex and arXiv within a fixed time window; 85 of 388 candidates were evaluated, so relevant studies in other databases, grey literature or non-English sources may be missing.

- Nearly all evidence on productivity and quality is self-reported perception from small, single-institution or mixed/undergraduate samples; no study provides objective time-on-task, output or blinded quality measures for graduate researchers.

- The final critic flagged two major unresolved issues: rq2 (research quality) is effectively unanswered, and the measurement half of rq6 (how AI-assisted graduate research productivity and quality are measured, plus graduate- or thesis-specific policy) has no direct evidence.

- rq3 and rq4 are thin: the only graduate-level usage-pattern-to-outcome link is a 10-person doctoral study of one task and tool, and moderators (AI literacy, English proficiency, discipline, supervisor guidance) come mostly from undergraduate, general-student or programming samples.

- Two claims are weakly supported: the 'postgraduates are more aware of limitations than undergraduates' finding rests on one single-university study, and the benefits-vs-challenges conflict pairs just two small studies; the absence of graduate evidence on literature screening and coding reflects only the current search.
