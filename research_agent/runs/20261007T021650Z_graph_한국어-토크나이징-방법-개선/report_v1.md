# Research Brief: 한국어 토크나이징 방법 개선

## 요약
397편의 후보 중 93편을 평가했으며, 한국어에 직접 해당하는 근거는 중간 신뢰도의 실증 연구 소수(주로 단일 과제)로 한정되고 효과 크기는 알 수 없다. 합의된 점은 형태소 사전 분절 후 서브워드 학습(morpheme+BPE)이 한국어 NLU·MT 과제 대부분에서 가장 강하고, 서브워드 단위가 단어·형태소 단위보다 OOV에 강건하다는 것이다. 터키어 등 형태소가 풍부한 언어의 연구도 이를 간접적으로 뒷받침한다. 다만 서브워드로도 CJK의 OOV 문제는 사라지지 않으며, 음절 단위 모델링이나 음절 패턴 기반 미등록어 추정이 띄어쓰기 오류와 미등록 형태소에 강건하다는 태깅 연구가 있다. 상충하는 지점은 형태소 분절이 사전 분절기로 쓰일 때와 독립 토크나이저로 쓰일 때의 결과가 다르다는 것이며, 과제·도메인·어휘 조건이 제각각이라 교란되어 있다(KorQuAD에서는 plain BPE가 우세, 리뷰 감성 분석에서는 독립 형태소 토큰이 열세). 핵심 Gap은 첫째, 한국어에서 fertility·형태소 경계 정렬·OOV rate 같은 내재적 지표를 KLUE·KoBEST 등 하위 과제 성능과 연결한 연구를 찾지 못했다는 점이다. 둘째, 어휘 크기와 단일언어/다국어 구성의 영향, 그리고 어휘 확장(EEVE)의 효율 효과가 한국어에서는 검증되지 않았다(리더보드 성능만 보고됨). 노이즈 텍스트 강건성과 자모 단위 토크나이저 비교도 비어 있다.

## 1. 주제 재정의
- RQ: How can Korean tokenization methods be improved to yield better tokenization quality and downstream NLP performance than existing approaches?
- 독립변수: improved Korean tokenization method (e.g., morpheme-aware or modified subword segmentation)
- 종속변수: tokenization quality, downstream NLP task performance, vocabulary efficiency (token count, sequence length)
- 대상: Korean-language text corpora and Korean NLP models
- 핵심 개념: Korean tokenization, morphological analysis, subword tokenization, byte-pair encoding, agglutinative language, Korean natural language processing, vocabulary design

## 2. 조사 계획
전략: Search 2016–present, with emphasis on 2019 onward, covering the transformer and LLM era, using OpenAlex and arXiv, and ACL Anthology-type venues surfaced through them. Prefer empirical studies with comparative baselines on Korean benchmarks (e.g., KLUE, NSMC, KorQuAD) and tokenization analyses; exclude papers that are only about Chinese or Japanese segmentation unless they offer transferable methods for agglutinative languages. I expect rq4 (intrinsic evaluation of Korean tokenization) and rq6 (robustness) to be the thinnest in the literature.}

- **rq1** How do morpheme-aware tokenization methods, including hybrid morphological analysis plus subword approaches, compare with purely statistical subword tokenizers on Korean downstream NLP task performance?  
  쿼리: morpheme-aware subword tokenization Korean; Korean morphological analysis BPE hybrid tokenizer; morpheme-based tokenizer Korean downstream tasks; Korean pre-trained language model morphological segmentation
- **rq2** How do standard subword algorithms (BPE, WordPiece, SentencePiece unigram, byte-level) behave on Korean agglutinative morphology and Hangul script, and what modifications to them have been proposed?  
  쿼리: byte-pair encoding agglutinative language Korean; SentencePiece WordPiece Korean comparison; Hangul jamo-level subword tokenization; byte-level tokenization Korean Unicode
- **rq3** How do vocabulary design choices (vocabulary size, composition, multilingual vs. monolingual vocabularies) affect Korean token efficiency, sequence length and model performance?  
  쿼리: vocabulary size effect Korean language model; multilingual tokenizer Korean token fertility; tokenizer vocabulary expansion Korean LLM; token count sequence length Korean text efficiency; Korean BERT vocabulary size ablation downstream accuracy; monolingual versus multilingual vocabulary Korean pretrained model; Korean tokens per character tokenizer vocabulary comparison
- **rq4** What intrinsic metrics and evaluation protocols are used to assess Korean tokenization quality, and how well do they correlate with downstream performance?  
  쿼리: intrinsic evaluation tokenization quality morphological alignment; Korean tokenizer evaluation benchmark; tokenization metrics correlation downstream performance; Korean word segmentation spacing evaluation; Korean tokenizer morpheme boundary alignment downstream KLUE; tokenizer fertility compression Korean KoBEST evaluation; Korean subword tokenization ablation intrinsic extrinsic correlation
- **rq5** How are tokenization improvements for Korean adapted in large language models, including tokenizer replacement or vocabulary extension in pre-trained models and the effects on cost and generation quality?  
  쿼리: Korean large language model tokenizer adaptation; vocabulary extension cross-lingual transfer Korean; tokenizer-free or byte-level models Korean; language model tokenizer inference cost Korean; Korean vocabulary expansion continual pretraining LLaMA; embedding initialization new tokens Korean LLM adaptation; Korean-centric LLM tokenizer extension token efficiency
- **rq6** What robustness issues and unintended effects do Korean tokenizers exhibit, such as handling of spacing errors, noisy user-generated text, out-of-vocabulary words, and rare morphological forms?  
  쿼리: Korean noisy text tokenization robustness; spacing errors Korean subword tokenizer; out-of-vocabulary Korean user-generated text; Korean social media morphological analysis

## 4. 종합
### 합의
- Morphological pre-segmentation followed by subword learning is the strongest option on most Korean NLU and MT tasks tested, and subword units beat word- or morpheme-level units on OOV robustness; the Korean evidence is two moderate-reliability empirical studies, so the effect sizes are unknown. [10.48550/arxiv.2010.02534, 10.3390/electronics10070845]
- Outside Korean, morphology-aware tokenization is competitive with or better than standard tokenizers on morphologically rich languages (Turkish) and generalizes better to OOV words (English-centric models); this is indirect support for Korean. [10.1145/3578707, 10.48550/arxiv.2404.13292]
- Subword tokenization does not eliminate OOV problems for Korean and other CJK languages, and OOV hurts transfer learning; Korean-specific handling such as syllable-level modeling or syllable-pattern estimation of unknown morphemes keeps tagging accuracy high under spacing errors and unknown morphemes (97-98 points). [10.2197/ipsjjip.29.490, 10.1145/3591206, 10.1162/089120102317341774]
- The same content can need many times more tokens in some languages than in others, even with multilingual tokenizers, implying unequal API cost, latency and context capacity; the abstracts do not single out Korean. [10.48550/arxiv.2305.15425, 10.48550/arxiv.2305.13707]
- Vocabulary extension of an English-centric LLM improves target-language proficiency with modest continued training. For Korean, the evidence is leaderboard proficiency from EEVE (about 2B training tokens, no efficiency numbers reported). The encoding-efficiency gain (20,000 added Chinese tokens) is shown only for Chinese LLaMA. [10.48550/arxiv.2402.14714, 10.48550/arxiv.2304.08177]
### 상충
- **Whether morpheme-based segmentation helps or hurts when used as a pre-segmenter versus as a standalone tokenizer for Korean.**
  - A: Morphological segmentation used as a pre-segmenter before BPE gave the best results on Korean-English MT and four NLU tasks, though plain BPE won on KorQuAD. [10.48550/arxiv.2010.02534]
  - B: Standalone morpheme-based tokenization lost to learned subwords: SentencePiece beat Mecab-Ko for review sentiment classification, and subword units were more OOV-robust than morpheme units in sentence-embedding sentiment analysis. [10.1080/08839514.2023.2175112, 10.3390/electronics10070845]
  - 가설: The contrasts differ: morphemes feeding BPE keep subword coverage for rare forms, whereas standalone morpheme tokens leave OOVs and jargon in noisy review text. The tasks also differ (MT/NLU benchmarks vs. sentiment on reviews), and the sentiment studies are single-task and single-domain with unstated sample sizes.
### 조건부
- Replacing whitespace with a special symbol hurt Korean sentiment analysis in one study; whether this is task-dependent is untested because only sentiment was evaluated. [10.3390/electronics10070845]
- In Turkish, larger vocabularies helped morphological- and word-level tokenizers more than de facto tokenizers; Korean applicability is untested, so confidence for Korean is low. [10.1145/3578707]
- In controlled European-language experiments, multilingual tokenizers needed roughly three times larger vocabularies than English ones, and fertility and parity did not always predict downstream performance; transfer to Korean is untested. [10.48550/arxiv.2310.08754]
- Pronunciation-based sub-character tokenization shortens sequences and resists homophone typos while staying competitive on Chinese PLMs; whether Hangul benefits is not shown. [10.1162/tacl_a_00560]

커버리지: The Korean evidence is thin on rq4: no Korean study links intrinsic metrics such as fertility, boundary alignment or OOV rate to downstream scores, and the correlation evidence is Turkish (10.48550/arxiv.2502.07057), European (10.48550/arxiv.2310.08754) and English (10.48550/arxiv.2404.13292); KLUE (10.48550/arxiv.2105.09680) and the Sejong study (10.1587/transinf.2017edp7085) never connect tokenization metrics to downstream results. rq3 is similarly unanswered for Korean, since the Korean items are an ASR corpus (10.3390/app10196936), a multi-language OOV paper (10.2197/ipsjjip.29.490) and a report without efficiency numbers (10.48550/arxiv.2402.14714); vocabulary-size evidence is Turkish or European. rq1 and rq2 have the most direct Korean evidence (10.48550/arxiv.2010.02534, 10.3390/electronics10070845, 10.1080/08839514.2023.2175112), but these are moderate-reliability, mostly single-task studies with no effect sizes. rq5 rests on one Korean technical report with leaderboard evaluation plus Chinese and cross-language cost studies. rq6 has spacing and unknown-morpheme evidence from POS tagging and NER but little on noisy user-generated text. Missing are Korean ablations varying vocabulary size and monolingual vs. multilingual vocabularies with tokens-per-character and downstream scores, KLUE/KoBEST tokenizer ablations with intrinsic metrics, and Korean LLM tokenizer cost studies.

## 5. Research Gap
- **No Korean study reports intrinsic tokenizer metrics (fertility, morpheme-boundary alignment, OOV rate) next to downstream scores. The correlation evidence comes only from Turkish and European languages, where the metrics predict downstream results inconsistently. Whether any metric predicts Korean downstream performance is unknown.** [10.48550/arxiv.2502.07057, 10.48550/arxiv.2310.08754, 10.48550/arxiv.2404.13292]
  - 제안 RQ: Which intrinsic tokenizer metrics (fertility, morpheme-boundary F1, OOV rate, token purity) predict KLUE and KoBEST downstream scores across Korean tokenizers?
  - 방법: Pretrain about 12 identical small RoBERTa models that differ only in tokenizer (BPE, Unigram, morpheme+BPE, jamo-level, byte-level), fine-tune on KLUE/KoBEST with 3 seeds, and compute rank correlations and mixed-effects regressions of task score on each metric. The main outcome is the correlation between each metric and downstream score, benchmarked against the Turkish %TR result.
  - 데이터: Korean corpora such as Modu or Namuwiki/CC-100 Korean (at least 10B characters) and the public KLUE and KoBEST suites. Compute is about 12 pretraining runs at small scale.
- **Korean vocabulary design (size, monolingual vs multilingual composition) is unstudied. The vocabulary-size evidence concerns Turkish morphological tokenizers and European multilingual tokenizers, and the only Korean sub-word size analysis is in ASR. Nothing links Korean vocabulary size to tokens per character and downstream accuracy.** [10.1145/3578707, 10.48550/arxiv.2310.08754, 10.3390/app10196936]
  - 제안 RQ: How do vocabulary size and monolingual vs multilingual composition affect tokens per Korean character and KLUE accuracy?
  - 방법: Run a controlled ablation with vocabulary sizes of 16k, 32k, 64k and 128k, each as a Korean-only and a Korean+English+Chinese+Japanese tokenizer, in 8 pretraining runs at fixed compute. Report tokens per character and KLUE scores, and test a size-by-composition interaction with seeds.
  - 데이터: Balanced Korean and multilingual web corpora (e.g., CC-100 or mC4 subsets) plus KLUE. The 8 runs fit a small-model compute budget.
- **The pre-segmenter vs standalone morpheme question is confounded across studies. Morpheme+BPE won on MT and NLU while plain BPE won on KorQuAD, but standalone morpheme tokenization lost on sentiment, where each study used different tasks, domains and vocabularies. Whitespace handling was tested only on sentiment.** [10.48550/arxiv.2010.02534, 10.3390/electronics10070845, 10.1080/08839514.2023.2175112]
  - 제안 RQ: With vocabulary size and training data fixed, does morpheme pre-segmentation before BPE outperform both standalone morpheme tokens and plain BPE, and does whitespace handling matter outside sentiment?
  - 방법: Pretrain 2x3 models (whitespace marker vs none; BPE, morpheme+BPE, standalone morpheme) and fine-tune on a pre-registered suite of NLI, NER, QA, sentiment and MT. Compare pairwise with paired bootstrap, and report per-task effect sizes with a task-by-tokenizer interaction. The KLUE suite serves as the evaluation set only.
  - 데이터: One shared Korean corpus, Mecab-Ko or Kiwi for morpheme segmentation, and KLUE, KorQuAD and NSMC, plus a noisy-review test set.
- **Korean vocabulary-extension evidence is leaderboard proficiency only, with no efficiency numbers. EEVE reports no tokens-per-character or latency, and the encoding-efficiency gain is shown only for Chinese LLaMA. The roles of added-vocabulary size, initialization, parameter freezing and training-token budget are not separated, and cost disparity is documented across languages without Korean figures.** [10.48550/arxiv.2402.14714, 10.48550/arxiv.2304.08177, 10.48550/arxiv.2305.15425]
  - 제안 RQ: How much does each of added-vocabulary size, embedding initialization, freezing schedule and training-token budget contribute to Korean encoding efficiency and task accuracy in an English-centric LLM?
  - 방법: Use a one-factor-at-a-time design around a baseline (10k added tokens, subword-mean initialization, staged freezing, 2B tokens), varying vocabulary size (3 levels), initialization (1 alternative), freezing (1 alternative) and budget (2 levels) for about 8 runs. Outcomes are tokens per Korean character, inference latency and KoBEST/KMMLU accuracy against the unextended model.
  - 데이터: A 7-10B open LLM, Korean continued-pretraining corpora (about 0.5-4B tokens), and parallel Korean-English text to measure token-length ratios.
- **Robustness of Korean tokenizers to noisy text and Hangul sub-character units has not been compared. The spacing- and unknown-morpheme-robust taggers are intrinsic tagging studies, not tokenizer comparisons, and the review-text evidence compares only Mecab-Ko with SentencePiece. Pronunciation-based sub-character tokenization is shown only for Chinese.** [10.1145/3591206, 10.1162/tacl_a_00560, 10.1080/08839514.2023.2175112]
  - 제안 RQ: Do jamo-level or syllable-pattern-aware tokenizers hold up better than BPE and morpheme+BPE under spacing errors, typos and slang in Korean user-generated text?
  - 방법: Fine-tune matched PLMs with BPE, morpheme+BPE, syllable-level and jamo-level tokenizers on sentiment and NER, and evaluate on clean text and on test sets with synthetic spacing and typo noise at 10/20/30% rates. Compare accuracy degradation curves between tokenizers.
  - 데이터: NSMC, KLUE-NER and a newly collected annotated set of about 3,000 community or review posts, with noise injected programmatically.

## 7. 한계와 신뢰도
- Only abstracts and metadata were read, not full texts, so effect sizes, sample sizes, hyperparameters and experimental controls could not be verified; claims rest on what authors chose to report in abstracts.
- The quality gate rated rq4 (intrinsic metrics vs. downstream scores) and rq3 (vocabulary size/composition) as effectively unanswered for Korean after two replans; the correlation and vocabulary-size evidence comes from Turkish, European-language and English-centric studies, so transfer to Korean is untested.
- The direct Korean evidence for rq1/rq2 is a handful of moderate-reliability, mostly single-task studies (MT/NLU benchmarks, review sentiment) using different tasks, domains and vocabularies, so the pre-segmenter vs. standalone morpheme conflict is confounded and cannot be resolved from this evidence.
